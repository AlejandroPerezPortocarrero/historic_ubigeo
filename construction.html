How the datasets are built

Goal

ubigeocensos records the presence of each department, province and district of Peru in the national population censuses of 1981, 1993, 2007, 2017 and 2025, together with each unit’s UBIGEO code and its date and legal instrument of creation. The aim is to make it easier to link census data across years, for example when building district panels that have to account for newly created districts.

The reference frame is the political-administrative division of the 2025 Census. The 2025 INEI UBIGEO is the unique identifier.

The datasets are built by data-raw/make_datasets.R, which runs the scripts in data-raw/ in order.

INEI (1999): Directorio Nacional de Municipalidades

For each department, the 1999 directory has a table titled “Dispositivo legal de creación, población total, superficie, densidad poblacional y población electoral de los distritos: 1999”. For Lima it is table 15.3 (https://proyectos.inei.gob.pe/web/biblioineipub/bancopub/Est/Lib0362/dis-lima.htm), and the other departments follow the pattern dis-<code>.htm (dis-amaz.htm, …, dis-ucay.htm). For each district the table gives the type, number and date of the legal instrument, or EPOCA INDEP. for districts dating from the Independence era. It also gives population (1998), surface, density and electoral population (1999). It covers only districts that existed in 1999.

INEI (2020): Proyecciones de Población 2018–2020, chapter 2

Chapter 2, “División política y administrativa al 31 de diciembre del 2019”, contains:

  1. the number of departments, provinces and districts at each census date, 1940–2017, and at 31/12/2019;
  2. the number of provinces and districts by department at each census;
  3. the provinces created between 12/07/1981 and 31/12/2019, with law number, publication date and UBIGEO;
  4. the districts created 1981–2019, with law number, year and territorial origin (the districts each one was carved from);
  5. a map of the districts created 1981–2019.

Table 4 is read from the word coordinates of the PDF, because its columns are right-aligned and some cells wrap over two lines. The few wrapped cells are fixed by hand in 03_inei2020_division.R.

INEI (2022): Proyecciones de Población Total 2018–2022, Anexo 2

Anexo 2 of Boletín Especial Nº 27, “División política y administrativa al 31 de diciembre de 2021”, updates the four tables above to 31/12/2021:

  • Cuadros 1 and 2 add the counts at 31/12/2021: 196 provinces and 1,890 districts.
  • Cuadro 3 lists the same 43 provinces, because none were created in 2020–2021.
  • Cuadro 4 adds rows 195–210, the 16 districts created in 2020–2021, with their law and territorial origin. It also renumbers the earlier rows consecutively, since INEI (2020) had skipped numbers 161–169.

These tables were provided as page images, which are kept in data-raw/inei2022_be27/images/. Cuadros 1 and 2 and the new rows of Cuadro 4 were transcribed into CSV files in the same folder. Every transcribed figure that INEI (2020) also reports is checked against the values parsed from the INEI (2020) PDF, and the build stops if any differs. All of them agree.

The two editions are combined in division_counts, inei_provinces_created, inei_districts_created and inei_district_origins. A source column says which edition(s) report each row.

Districts created after 2019

The 2025 frame has 1,892 districts, against 1,874 at 31/12/2019 (INEI 2020) and 1,890 at 31/12/2021 (INEI 2022). Of the 18 districts created in between:

  • the 16 created in 2020–2021 are listed in INEI (2022), with year and law. Their exact dates come from JNE Resolución N.° 0913-2021-JNE, and the build checks that both sources agree on law and year;
  • the two created after 2021, Alto Trujillo (Ley 31644, 2022) and Santa Rosa de Loreto (Ley 32403, 2025), come from the Congreso’s list of district-creation laws.

Step 1: units in the 2025 Census

Script: data-raw/01_units_2025.R.

sapply(list(departments = departments, provinces = provinces, districts = districts), nrow)
#> departments   provinces   districts 
#>          25         196        1892

Checks run by the script:

  • UBIGEO codes are unique;
  • every district has its province and every province has its department;
  • no province is left without districts.

Step 2: creation dates and instruments

Creation data come from four sources, applied in this order:

  1. INEI 1999 gives the exact date and instrument for each district that existed in 1999. So far this is Lima, matched by department, province and normalised name.
  2. INEI 2020 / INEI 2022 give the publication date and law for the 43 provinces created 1981–2021, and the year and law for the 210 districts created 1981–2021. When INEI 1999 also has the district, its exact date is kept and the law numbers are checked against each other.
  3. JNE 2021 / Congreso 2025 give the exact date and law for the 18 districts created 2020–2025.

Districts with no creation source (NA) existed before the 1981 Census but their creation date is not yet in the package. Adding the INEI 1999 tables for the other 24 departments will fill most of them.

Values that look wrong in the sources are kept as published and flagged:

  • INEI 1999: Veintisiete de Noviembre (Huaral) has Law 17702 with a 1957 date (probably Law 12702).
  • INEI 2020: Datem del Marañón appears with Law 228593 in the province table and Law 28593 in the district table.
  • INEI 2020: two origin districts cannot be matched: “Acobamba” (Chincheros) and “Camilaca” (Azángaro). Camilaca is a district of Candarave, Tacna.
  • INEI 2020: one origin row gives “Huaylas” as the department of Huaylas (Áncash).

Step 3: presence in each census

A unit is coded as present in a census when it had been created before the census date: 12/07/1981, 11/07/1993, 21/10/2007 or 22/10/2017. Units not listed as created between 1981 and 2025 existed before 1981. A district created in a census year with no exact date (1981 or 1993) is coded as created after that census.

This rule reproduces exactly INEI’s national counts of provinces and districts at every census. It also reproduces the counts at 31/12/2021 (INEI 2022), nationally and in every department. The build script stops if any of these checks fails.

check <- data.frame(
  census = c(1981, 1993, 2007, 2017),
  provinces_pkg = sapply(c(1981, 1993, 2007, 2017), function(y) sum(provinces[[paste0("census_", y)]])),
  districts_pkg = sapply(c(1981, 1993, 2007, 2017), function(y) sum(districts[[paste0("census_", y)]])))
nat <- subset(division_counts, level == "national" & census_year %in% check$census)
check$provinces_inei <- nat$n_provinces
check$districts_inei <- nat$n_districts
check
#>   census provinces_pkg districts_pkg provinces_inei districts_inei
#> 1   1981           153          1680            153           1680
#> 2   1993           188          1793            188           1793
#> 3   2007           195          1833            195           1833
#> 4   2017           196          1874            196           1874

At 31/12/2021 the agreement also holds department by department:

d21 <- subset(division_counts, date == as.Date("2021-12-31") & level == "department")
d21$dist_pkg <- sapply(d21$inei, function(d) sum(substr(districts$inei, 1, 2) == substr(d, 1, 2) &
                                                 districts$created_year %in% c(NA, 1800:2021)))
all(d21$n_districts == d21$dist_pkg)
#> [1] TRUE

At census dates, the department counts differ from INEI’s in a few department-years:

dc <- subset(division_counts, level == "department" & census_year %in% c(1981, 1993, 2007, 2017))
dc$prov_pkg <- mapply(function(d, y) sum(substr(provinces$inei, 1, 2) == substr(d, 1, 2) &
                                         provinces[[paste0("census_", y)]] == 1), dc$inei, dc$census_year)
dc$dist_pkg <- mapply(function(d, y) sum(substr(districts$inei, 1, 2) == substr(d, 1, 2) &
                                         districts[[paste0("census_", y)]] == 1), dc$inei, dc$census_year)
subset(as.data.frame(dc), n_provinces != prov_pkg | n_districts != dist_pkg,
       c(department, census_year, n_provinces, prov_pkg, n_districts, dist_pkg))
#>      department census_year n_provinces prov_pkg n_districts dist_pkg
#> 17     Amazonas        2007           7        7          84       83
#> 23       Áncash        1981          16       16         154      155
#> 59    Cajamarca        1981          11       11         109      107
#> 95      Huánuco        1981           7        7          68       67
#> 122 La Libertad        1981           7        7          72       74
#> 149      Loreto        1981           5        6          37       43
#> 230     Ucayali        1981           2        1          15        9

The differences cancel out across departments and come from two causes:

  • Units that later moved to another department. The package counts each unit in its 2025 department. For example, the province of Ucayali and its six districts belonged to the department of Ucayali in 1981 and are in Loreto today.
  • An inconsistency in INEI (2020) itself. For 2007 the department table sums to 1,834 districts, while the national table reports 1,833 (Amazonas: 84 against 83). The difference is the district of Bagua, which the district table lists under 2008.

Interpretation. census_YYYY refers to the unit as identified in 2025. A district that was later split still counts as present in earlier censuses, but with a larger territory; inei_district_origins identifies those parent districts. Units abolished before 2025 are not in the frame.
